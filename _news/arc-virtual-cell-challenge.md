Happy to share that our team ranked **14th in the Arc Virtual Cell Challenge**. The challenge, organized by the [Arc Institute](https://arcinstitute.org/news/virtual-cell-challenge-2025-wrap-up) and presented at NeurIPS 2025, brought together more than 5,000 participants from 114 countries to develop models that predict cellular responses to genetic perturbations.

I teamed up with Philipp Sven Lars Schäfer and Martin Garrido Rodriguez-Cordoba at the [Saez Lab](https://saezlab.org/). We are proud of what we achieved with very limited compute and no access to private data: our model was lightweight, trained in minutes, and gave us the flexibility to iterate quickly across many ideas.

One of the main lessons from the competition was how difficult it remains to define biologically meaningful and robust metrics for perturbation prediction. During the challenge, we found that the proposed metrics, while well intentioned, had important limitations that made reliable model assessment challenging. This is an important open problem for the field, and one that will shape how virtual cell models are developed and compared. Two interesting posts discussing these issues are [The state of research on virtual cells](https://behindbioml.substack.com/p/the-state-of-research-on-virtual) and [Virtual cell perturbation metrics](https://giovannipalla.substack.com/p/virtual-cell-perturbation-metrics).

Thanks to Julio Saez Rodriguez for supporting and encouraging us during the challenge, and to the Arc Institute for organizing it.
